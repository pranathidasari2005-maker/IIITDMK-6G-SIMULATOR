Experiment 8 — Communication + Computing Load Evaluation
Research question: How does the 6G-LENA framework handle increasing concurrent service load when communication and computing resources are jointly required?

Measured results:
- One to five offered sessions were all successfully established.
- Communication and computing utilization increased from 20% at one session to 100% at five sessions.
- At six offered sessions, five sessions succeeded and one failed, giving an 83.33% success rate.
- At seven offered sessions, five sessions succeeded and two failed, giving a 71.43% success rate.
- At eight offered sessions, five sessions succeeded and three failed, giving a 62.50% success rate.
- Both modeled resources reached full utilization at five concurrent sessions.
- All resources were restored to full availability after each load-level test.

The results show the expected capacity-limited behavior of the modeled communication and computing resources under increasing concurrent service load. The observed saturation point is specific to the configured 100-unit capacities and 20-unit per-session requirements and should not be interpreted as a physical 6G capacity limit.

Experiment 10 — Mobility / Handover
============================================================

Research question:
Can the 6G-LENA framework maintain service-session consistency and
resource correctness when a UE changes its serving access node?

Scenario:
A UE initially connects to AccessNode-1 through EdgeNode-1. A service
session requiring 20 communication resource units and 20 computing
resource units is activated. The session is then released, the UE is
connected to AccessNode-2 through EdgeNode-2, and a new service session
is activated.

Measured validation results:
- Old session release: PASS
- New AccessNode-2 connection: PASS
- New session activation: PASS
- Old communication resource restored: PASS
- New communication resource reserved: PASS

Validation success:
5/5 checks passed (100%).

Interpretation:
The experiment demonstrates that the implemented network-node and
service-session abstractions can represent a controlled access-node
handover while maintaining resource consistency. The original
communication resource was restored after release, while the new access
node correctly reserved the required communication resource for the
new session.

This result validates the implemented handover workflow for the tested
simulation scenario. It does not represent physical 6G mobility
performance or imply universal handover reliability.
